The TRUST index

Method last updated 9 October 2026

The TRUST index gives every new development in Marrakech a grade of A or B, based on five verifiable criteria. Where the file does not yet allow a grade, the development is marked “Incomplete assessment”, which is not a grade. Three criteria are knock-out criteria: until the land title, the planning permit and the off-plan (VEFA) safeguards are verified, a development gets neither A nor B. The grade cannot be bought.

The grid: five criteria, T-R-U-S-T

Each criterion rests on a document the developer can produce and we can check.

CriterionWhat we checkApplies toRole in the grade
T — Title deedLand title established, no registered disputeDevelopmentKnock-out
R — Track recordDelivered projects, deadlines metDeveloperNeeded for A
U — Urban planningBuilding or subdivision permitDevelopmentKnock-out
S — Off-plan safeguardsCompliant preliminary contract, refund or completion guaranteeDevelopmentKnock-out
T — TransparencyPlans, specification, published prices, replies to requestsDeveloperNeeded for A

VEFA is the off-plan sale (vente en l'état futur d'achèvement), governed in Morocco by law 107-12. Each criterion applies either to a specific development or to its developer, as the “Applies to” column shows.

The knock-out criteria

Three criteria are knock-out criteria: the land title (T), the building or subdivision permit (U) and the off-plan safeguards (S). If any of the three is not verified, the development gets neither A nor B.

This is deliberate: a developer's reputation does not make up for a plot whose title is not established, for a missing permit, or for a contract that does not protect an off-plan buyer. Track record (R) and transparency (T) only separate A from B.

What A, B and “Incomplete assessment” mean

A: the three knock-out criteria are verified, and the developer's track record and transparency are verified too. B: the three knock-out criteria are verified, but at least one of the other two (track record, transparency) is not. “Incomplete assessment”, which is not a grade: at least one knock-out criterion is not verified.

A
The development's land title, permit and off-plan safeguards are verified, and so are the developer's track record and transparency.
B
The development's land title, permit and off-plan safeguards are verified, but the developer's track record or transparency (either one, or both) is not verified yet. B is a real grade: the basic safeguards are verified.
Incomplete assessment
At least one of the three knock-out criteria is not verified, or the development's developer is not yet in our records. It is not a grade: on the date shown, the file does not allow us to say A or B.
  • A criterion is “verified” when we have obtained and checked the corresponding document. Any other state (not verified, unknown) counts as not verified.
  • There is no grade below B: KEYPLAN publishes no C or D.
  • A developer's grade is the lowest of the grades of its developments listed on KEYPLAN. A developer with no listed development, or with a development whose assessment is incomplete, shows “Incomplete assessment”.

Weighting

No numerical weighting is applied at present: the grade follows from the rules above. If a weighting is introduced, it will be published on this page before it is used.

The process

Every grade is first sent to the developer as a pre-grade. The developer has 15 days to reply, then the grade is published, together with the reply as written if there is one.

  1. Collection. For each criterion, we gather the relevant documents: those that are public and those the developer gives us.
  2. Pre-grade. We send the developer the pre-grade criterion by criterion, showing what is verified and what is missing.
  3. Reply within 15 days. The developer can complete the file, correct a mistake or contest a point.
  4. Publication. The grade is published with its last-checked date. The developer's right of reply appears on its page, published as is, without any edit from us.
  5. Quarterly review. Every quarter we review the published grades and update the last-checked date.

Sources we consult

We rely on documents, not on statements: what is not documented stays “not verified”. The exact documents depend on the development.

  • the land title, or an up-to-date extract issued by the land registry authority;
  • the building or subdivision permit issued by the municipality, and its conditions;
  • the preliminary contract offered to buyers and the guarantee that comes with it;
  • the documents that identify the company: trade register, articles of association, history;
  • for the track record, the developer's projects already delivered: announced and actual delivery dates, as documents establish them;
  • the published plans, specification and price list, and how the developer answers our requests.

Price is not part of the grade

Price is not part of the grade: none of the five criteria looks at the price level.

A low or high price informs, it does not rate. A development can be rated A and cost more than its neighbours, or be incomplete and cost less. Price benchmarks (starting prices and averages of the listed developments, district by district) are on the page new-build prices in Marrakech.

Independence: the grade cannot be bought

No developer can buy or negotiate its grade. The only way to change it is to provide documents we can verify; no advertising, partnership or payment enters the calculation.

KEYPLAN and the INSIDER Properties agency share the same founder. INSIDER only sells developments rated A or B. The rating cannot be bought.

A rating portal and an agency that share a founder is a possible conflict of interest. To limit it, the rating rules are public on this page, KEYPLAN also lists the developments INSIDER does not sell, and INSIDER sticks to developments rated A or B. Meet the agency: INSIDER Properties.

The “Rated A on TRUST” badge

A developer rated A can display the “Rated A on TRUST” badge on its own site, free of charge, if it wishes.

The badge is optional and free. It links to the developer's page on KEYPLAN, where the grade is shown with its last-checked date, so readers always see the current grade. It must only stay up while the page shows A; if the grade changes, it must be removed.
