The TRUST score: how a development's file is assessed

The TRUST score rates out of 100 the file of every new development in Marrakech, based on eleven verifiable items grouped into five criteria. If any of the three vital items is not verified, there is no score: the development is marked “Incomplete file”.

The grid: five criteria, 100 points

The score adds up the points of eleven items grouped into five criteria. Each criterion is weighted by the risk to an off-plan buyer: land title 25 points, track record 15, urban planning 20, off-plan safeguards 25, transparency 15.

The eleven items and their points

Each criterion is made of specific items whose points add up to its weight. Eight items are checked development by development; three (R1, R2 and X3) are checked once for the developer and count for each of its developments.

How the score is calculated

A verified item earns its full points, a partly verified item half of them, an item that is not verified or not disclosed none. The total is rounded to the nearest whole number, once, at the end.

  • Verified: the item's full points.

  • Partly verified: half the points.

  • Not verified or not disclosed: no points.

Half points are not rounded item by item: a criterion can stand at 7.5 out of 15. Only the total is rounded, with a half point going up (82.5 becomes 83).

Three vital items: without them, no score

The land title (T1), the building or subdivision permit (U1) and the completion guarantee or guarantee of refund of the sums paid (S2) are vital items. If any of them is not fully verified, the development has no score: it shows “Incomplete file”, with the vital item or items that are missing.

This is deliberate: points earned elsewhere do not make up for a plot whose title is not established, for a missing permit, or for an off-plan buyer left without a guarantee. No number is then published for the development, and it is listed after those that have a score.

The score tiers

From 80 to 100: “Excellent file”. From 65 to 79: “Good file”. From 0 to 64: “File to be completed”. “Incomplete file” is not a tier: it means there is no score. The score is always shown out of 100, with its tier and its last-checked date.

  • 80–100

    Excellent file

    Score from 80 to 100. All three vital items are verified.

  • 65–79

    Good file

    Score from 65 to 79. All three vital items are verified.

  • 0–64

    File to be completed

    Score from 0 to 64. All three vital items are verified; other items are still to be verified.

  • Incomplete file

    At least one vital item (land title, building or subdivision permit, completion or refund guarantee) is not verified: no score.

  • A developer's score is the rounded average of the scores of its developments that have one. If none of its developments has a score, the developer shows “Incomplete file”.
  • Developer ranking: by score, highest first, then by most recent check. Developers without a score are listed afterwards, unranked.
  • On each page, the score is broken down criterion by criterion (for example “T 25/25 · R 7.5/15 · U 20/20 · S 20/25 · T 10/15”), then item by item, with each item's state and points.
  • A score is never published without its last-checked date.

Why these weights

The weights follow the risk to an off-plan buyer: the land title and the off-plan safeguards, which protect the property and the sums paid, weigh 25 points each. Any change to the grid, the points or the tiers is published on this page, with its date.

The process

Every score is first sent to the developer as a pre-score. The developer has 15 days to reply; the score is then published, together with the reply as written if there is one, and reviewed every quarter.

  1. Collecting the documents

    First

    For each of the eleven items, we gather the relevant documents: those that are public and those the developer gives us.

  2. Pre-score sent to the developer

    Before publication

    We send the developer the pre-score item by item: what is verified, partly verified or missing, and the points that follow.

  3. 15days

    The developer's reply

    15 days

    The developer has 15 days to complete the file, correct a mistake or contest a point.

  4. Publication and right of reply

    Then

    The score is published with its last-checked date. The developer's reply appears on its page, published as is, without any edit from us.

  5. Review

    Every quarter

    We review the items behind the published scores and update the last-checked date.

Sources we consult

We rely on documents, not on statements: an item that is not documented stays “not verified” and earns no points. The exact documents depend on the development.

  • Land title (T1, T2): the title, or an up-to-date extract issued by the land registry authority, and a recent ownership certificate for registered disputes and charges.

  • Urban planning (U1, U2): the building or subdivision permit issued by the municipality and its conditions, compared with the development as marketed.

  • Off-plan safeguards (S1, S2): the preliminary contract offered to buyers, read against law 107-12, and the completion or refund guarantee document.

  • The documents that identify the company: trade register, articles of association, history. They link the title and the permit to the developer or its project company.

  • Track record (R1, R2): the developer's projects already delivered, with their announced and actual delivery dates, as documents establish them.

  • Transparency (X1 to X3): the published plans, specification and prices, and the documents the developer provides when we ask for them.

Price is not part of the score

None of the eleven items looks at the price level: item X2 only checks that prices are published.

A low or high price informs, it does not rate. A development can have an excellent file and cost more than its neighbours, or have an incomplete file and cost less. Price benchmarks (starting prices and averages of the listed developments, district by district) are on a separate page.

The TRUST badge

A developer with a score can, if it wishes, display the TRUST badge on its own site: its score out of 100, its tier and the last-checked date.

The badge is optional. It links to the developer's page on KEYPLAN, where the score is broken down item by item. It must show exactly the score, tier and date displayed on that page, be updated whenever they change, and be removed if the page no longer shows a score.
