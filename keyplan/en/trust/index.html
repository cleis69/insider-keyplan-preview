The TRUST index: how a development is graded

The TRUST index gives every new development in Marrakech a grade of A or B, based on five verifiable criteria. Where the file does not yet allow a grade, the development is marked “Incomplete assessment”, which is not a grade.

The grid: five criteria, T-R-U-S-T

Each criterion rests on a document the developer can produce and we can check. Three criteria are knock-out criteria, two are needed for an A.

  1. T

    T: Title deed

    Land title established, no registered dispute.

    Development
    Role in the grade
    Knock-out
  2. R

    R: Track record

    Delivered projects, deadlines met.

    Applies to
    Developer
    Role in the grade
    Needed for A
  3. U

    U: Urban planning

    Building or subdivision permit.

    Applies to
    Development
    Role in the grade
    Knock-out
  4. S

    S: Off-plan safeguards

    Compliant preliminary contract, refund or completion guarantee.

    Applies to
    Development
    Role in the grade
    Knock-out
  5. T

    T: Transparency

    Plans, specification, published prices, replies to requests.

    Applies to
    Developer
    Role in the grade
    Needed for A

VEFA is the off-plan sale (vente en l'état futur d'achèvement), governed in Morocco by law 107-12. Each criterion applies either to a specific development or to its developer.

The knock-out criteria

Three criteria are knock-out criteria: the land title (T), the building or subdivision permit (U) and the off-plan safeguards (S). If any of the three is not verified, the development gets neither A nor B.

This is deliberate: a developer's reputation does not make up for a plot whose title is not established, for a missing permit, or for a contract that does not protect an off-plan buyer. Track record (R) and transparency (T) only separate A from B.

What A, B and “Incomplete assessment” mean

A: the three knock-out criteria are verified, and the developer's track record and transparency are verified too. B: the three knock-out criteria are verified, but at least one of the other two is not. “Incomplete assessment” is not a grade: at least one knock-out criterion is not verified.

  • ATRUST

    Grade A

    Complete file

    The development's land title, permit and off-plan safeguards are verified, and so are the developer's track record and transparency.

  • BTRUST

    Grade B

    Knock-out criteria verified

    The development's land title, permit and off-plan safeguards are verified, but the developer's track record or transparency (either one, or both) is not verified yet. B is a real grade: the basic safeguards are verified.

  • TRUST

    Incomplete assessment

    Not a grade

    At least one of the three knock-out criteria is not verified, or the development's developer is not yet in our records. On the date shown, the file does not allow us to say A or B.

  • A criterion is “verified” when we have obtained and checked the corresponding document. Any other state (not verified, unknown) counts as not verified.
  • There is no grade below B: KEYPLAN publishes no C or D.
  • A developer's grade is the lowest of the grades of its developments listed on KEYPLAN. A developer with no listed development, or with a development whose assessment is incomplete, shows “Incomplete assessment”.

Weighting

No numerical weighting is applied at present: the grade follows from the rules above. If a weighting is introduced, it will be published on this page before it is used.

The process

Every grade is first sent to the developer as a pre-grade. The developer has 15 days to reply, then the grade is published, together with the reply as written if there is one.

  1. Collecting the documents

    First

    For each criterion, we gather the relevant documents: those that are public and those the developer gives us.

  2. Pre-grade sent to the developer

    Before publication

    We send the developer the pre-grade criterion by criterion, showing what is verified and what is missing.

  3. 15days

    The developer's reply

    15 days

    The developer has 15 days to complete the file, correct a mistake or contest a point.

  4. Publication and right of reply

    Then

    The grade is published with its last-checked date. The developer's reply appears on its page, published as is, without any edit from us.

  5. Review

    Every quarter

    We review the published grades and update the last-checked date.

Sources we consult

We rely on documents, not on statements: what is not documented stays “not verified”. The exact documents depend on the development.

  • The land title, or an up-to-date extract issued by the land registry authority.

  • The building or subdivision permit issued by the municipality, and its conditions.

  • The preliminary contract offered to buyers and the guarantee that comes with it.

  • The documents that identify the company: trade register, articles of association, history.

  • For the track record, the developer's projects already delivered: announced and actual delivery dates, as documents establish them.

  • The published plans, specification and price list, and how the developer answers our requests.

Price is not part of the grade

None of the five criteria looks at the price level.

A low or high price informs, it does not rate. A development can be rated A and cost more than its neighbours, or be incomplete and cost less. Price benchmarks (starting prices and averages of the listed developments, district by district) are on a separate page.

The “Rated A on TRUST” badge

A developer rated A can display the “Rated A on TRUST” badge on its own site, free of charge, if it wishes.

The badge is optional. It links to the developer's page on KEYPLAN, where the grade is shown with its last-checked date, so readers always see the current grade. It must only stay up while the page shows A; if the grade changes, it must be removed.
