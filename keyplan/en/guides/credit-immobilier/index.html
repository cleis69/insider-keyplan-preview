Loan for an off-plan purchase in Morocco: what to compare between lenders' offers

Type of rate, term, fees, borrower insurance, staged release of funds: a checklist of what to compare between two loan offers for an off-plan purchase, and the file to assemble before approaching banks.

How the financing works

You finance the purchase with your own contribution and, often, a loan from a bank, which studies your file and the property before giving its agreement.

The bank first looks at your ability to repay: income, stability of your employment, existing loans, savings. It then examines the property and the sale documents.

With a new-build off-plan, the property does not exist yet: the bank also looks at the development and the developer, and the loan is most often released as the works progress. Practices vary from bank to bank; ask yours before you sign anything.

The down payment

The down payment is the part of the price you fund from your own savings. The level required depends on each bank and on your profile, so the same purchase may be accepted by one bank and need a larger down payment at another.

Work it out on the whole project, not only on the unit price: deed and settling-in costs come on top and are not always covered by the loan (see the guide on purchase costs).

The bank, and sometimes the notary, may ask you to show where the down payment comes from. Keep the relevant evidence:

  • Savings account statements
  • Deed or proof of the sale of another property
  • Deed of gift or a written statement, where the money comes from a relative
  • Evidence for any other large payment

The bank file

A bank generally asks for identity documents, proof of income and employment, your account statements and the property documents. The exact list comes from the bank.

  • Identity document and proof of address
  • Proof of income and employment: payslips and an employer's letter, or accounts if you are self-employed
  • Recent account statements and details of your existing loans
  • Evidence of your down payment
  • Property documents: reservation or draft preliminary contract, plans, written specification and, if the bank asks, the land title and building permit

Put this file together before approaching several banks, so that you can compare them on the same basis. An agreement in principle is not a final offer: ask what it commits the bank to and for how long it remains valid.

Staged payment calls during construction

In an off-plan purchase the price is paid in stages tied to the progress of the works (payment calls), and the loan is most often released in tranches at the same pace. The details are to be confirmed with your bank.

Each payment call should correspond to a stage defined in the preliminary contract. Check that the schedule is in writing and that the stages are described, and ask how progress is recorded before each call.

During construction you may have to pay interest on the tranches already released while still paying for your current home. Build this into your budget: the terms depend on the offer.

Before signing the preliminary contract, ask the bank how it handles an off-plan purchase and whether it requires particular documents about the developer or the development.

Comparing loan offers

Compare the total cost of the loan, not just the rate: type of rate, term, fees, insurance, early repayment and how the construction phase is treated.

  • Type of rate (fixed or variable) and what could make it change
  • Term, monthly payment and the share that is interest
  • Arrangement and security fees
  • Borrower insurance: what it covers and what it costs
  • Early repayment and changes to the repayment schedule
  • How funds are released during construction

Ask each bank for a written simulation using the same amount and the same term. Rates change and depend on your profile: only a dated simulation tells you the rate actually on offer.

Points to watch before you sign

Do not sign a preliminary contract without knowing whether your financing is secured, and know what happens to your payments if the bank says no.

A loan refused after you have paid a deposit is the situation to plan for first. The points below help you guard against it.

  • Ask whether the contract or reservation can be made conditional on obtaining the loan, and what happens to your deposit if the loan is refused: to be confirmed with your notary.
  • Work out your own repayment capacity with your real outgoings rather than relying on the bank's calculation alone.
  • Allow for timing: processing of the file, approval, then the developer's schedule.
  • If you live abroad, the bank may ask for additional documents.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a down payment to buy off-plan with a loan?

The level required depends on the bank and on your profile, and it varies from one lender to another. Ask several banks, and remember that the deed costs come on top of the price.

Can I get a loan before signing the preliminary contract?

It depends on the bank: some study the file from a reservation or a draft contract, others wait for the signed contract. Ask your bank before paying anything.

Is the loan paid out in one go?

For a property under construction, funds are often released in tranches tied to the payment calls. This is to be confirmed in your bank's loan offer.

What if the bank refuses my file after I have reserved?

It depends on what your reservation document or contract says: does it contain a condition linked to financing, and is your deposit refundable? Have this spelled out before you pay, and check it with a notary.
