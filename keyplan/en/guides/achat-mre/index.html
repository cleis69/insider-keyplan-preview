Moroccans living abroad (MRE): documents, power of attorney and checks before buying in Morocco

KEYPLAN · Updated 9 October 2026

The steps, seen from abroad

You can usually organise the choice of development, the checking of documents and the follow-up of payments remotely; signing takes place in person or by power of attorney. What is possible for your purchase is to be confirmed with the notary before you reserve.

An MRE (Moroccan resident abroad) buying off-plan goes through the general process described in the VEFA guide. What changes is mostly practical: you cannot be present at every stage, so you need to arrange in advance who checks, who signs and who follows the works. Rules specific to your situation may also apply, particularly for payments: to be confirmed with your notary and your bank.

  • Choose: plans, a virtual tour, or a visit by a relative or a trusted representative.
  • Check: ask in writing for the land title, building permit and guarantee, and have a notary review them.
  • Reserve: terms in writing before any payment, and pay only by the means stated in the document.
  • Sign: in person during a stay, or by power of attorney (see below).
  • Pay: by traceable bank transfers, following the schedule in the contract.
  • Follow: progress reports, dated photos, or a visit by a representative.
  • Take handover: inspection by you or your representative, with a list of defects.

The power of attorney

A power of attorney authorises someone you trust to sign in your place for a specific transaction. From abroad, it is signed before the authority that the notary handling the purchase will specify, for example the Moroccan consulate in your country of residence.

Ask the notary for a template before going to the consulate or to a local notary: the wording, the competent authority, any legalisation and the number of original copies vary from case to case. Have the notary handling the purchase approve the text before you sign it.

  • Choose someone you trust who is independent of the seller: avoid the developer and its sales staff.
  • Limit the powers to the specific transaction and property rather than granting a general power.
  • Set a limited duration and know how to revoke it.
  • Allow time for the original to reach Morocco before the signing date.
  • Keep a copy and require written reports on whatever is signed in your name.

Transferring the funds, and being able to repatriate them one day

Send each payment by a traceable bank transfer to the account named in the contract, and keep every supporting document: they will also matter if you resell and wish to repatriate the proceeds.

  • Check that the destination account is the one in the contract and matches the name of the selling company (or the procedure given by the notary).
  • Avoid any cash payment or payment to a personal account.
  • One transfer per payment call, quoting the unit reference.
  • Keep the transfer orders, the currency exchange confirmations and any bank certificates for the transfers.
  • Allow for exchange and transfer fees, delays and movements in the exchange rate.

The exchange and transfer rules that apply to your situation, in particular if you may one day want to repatriate the proceeds of a resale, are to be confirmed with your bank and your notary. Keep proof of the origin of the funds and of their transfer from the start.

What to check before paying

The checks are the same as if you were there, but they matter more at a distance: land title, permit, guarantee, the seller's identity and the contract.

Do not rely on a virtual tour or a sales plan. Ask for the documents in writing and cross-check names and floor areas. KEYPLAN's TRUST grid shows, for each development, whether the land title, permit and off-plan guarantee are verified, not verified or not disclosed.

  • The land title and building permit, in the name of the right company.
  • The text of the guarantee and who issues it.
  • The preliminary contract, reviewed by a notary before signing.
  • A contact reachable in Morocco, with full contact details.

Your family situation and succession

Have the notary explain who needs to be involved at signing and what happens to the property in case of death or separation, before deciding in whose name to buy.

A property in Morocco often matters to the whole family, sometimes spread across several countries. These questions are easier to settle before the purchase than after it.

  • Depending on your marital property regime, does your spouse need to be present or give consent for the signing?
  • Will the property be bought in your name alone or jointly, and what are the consequences?
  • Who could inherit it, and what formalities would the heirs have to complete in Morocco and in your country of residence?
  • The civil status and family status documents the notary will require from you.

After signing: follow-up, handover and management

Decide in advance who will follow the works, who will take handover and who will look after the property once delivered, especially if you do not plan to live in it straight away.

During construction, ask for regular progress reports, preferably with dated photos, and match them to the stages in your payment schedule before each transfer.

At handover, a trusted representative can carry out the inspection in your place, if you have provided for it in a power of attorney. If the property stays empty or is let, decide who holds the keys, who pays the charges and who looks after maintenance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy in Morocco without travelling?

Much of the process can be handled remotely, but the deeds are signed either by you in person or through a power of attorney. What is possible for your purchase is to be confirmed with the notary before you reserve.

Where do I sign a power of attorney when I live abroad?

Before the authority that the notary handling the purchase will specify, for example the Moroccan consulate in your country of residence. Ask the notary for the template before you go, and allow time for the original to be sent.

Will I be able to repatriate the money if I resell?

The rules that apply to your situation are to be confirmed with your bank and your notary at the time of purchase, not at the time of resale. Keep the transfer orders, currency exchange confirmations and bank certificates from the outset.

Does my spouse need to be involved in the purchase?

It may depend on your marital property regime and on the name in which the property is bought. Put the question to the notary before you reserve, with your civil status documents.

Can an MRE get a loan in Morocco?

It may be possible with some banks, depending on your profile and your documents. Ask several banks what they require from a customer living abroad, and see the guide on financing.
