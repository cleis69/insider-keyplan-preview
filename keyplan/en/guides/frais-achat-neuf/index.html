Costs of buying a new-build in Morocco: what to budget for

KEYPLAN · Updated 9 October 2026

Advertised price and real budget

The advertised price is only part of the budget: add the deed and formalities costs, any financing costs, the cost of settling in, and the recurring running costs.

This guide sorts these costs by category because the amounts depend on the price, the type of property, your situation and the rules in force at the time of the sale. Ask your notary for a written breakdown before you commit.

To compare two developments, also compare what the price includes: parking, storage room, terrace, fitted kitchen, utility connections. Two identical prices can hide very different budgets. Finally, ask whether the advertised price is exclusive or inclusive of taxes.

Costs of the deed and formalities

They cover the notary's fees, the duties and taxes linked to registering the sale, and the fees for recording the property at the land registry (conservation foncière). The exact amounts are to be confirmed with your notary.

  • The notary's fees (or those of the professional who drafts the deeds)
  • Registration duties and taxes on the sale
  • Land registry (conservation foncière) recording fees
  • Copies, stamps, certificates and administrative documents
  • Translations and legalisations, if your documents come from abroad

Ask three questions: which of these costs fall due at reservation, at the preliminary contract and at the final deed; which are yours and which are the developer's; and when to pay them. Have the answers put in writing.

Financing costs

If you borrow, allow for the arrangement fees, borrower insurance, the costs of the security the bank takes on the property, and any other bank charges.

  • Bank arrangement fees
  • Borrower insurance: cover and cost vary from one offer to another
  • Fees for registering the security the bank takes on the property (a mortgage, for example)
  • Interest payable during construction on the tranches already released, depending on the offer
  • Costs of early repayment or of changing the loan, to be known before you sign

How the loan and the staged payment calls work is covered in the guide on financing an off-plan purchase.

Costs of settling in

Once you have the keys, allow for furniture, appliances, utility connections and subscriptions, small finishing jobs, and home insurance.

  • Furniture and decoration
  • Appliances, air conditioning, kitchen or bathroom where these are not supplied
  • Meter set-up and subscriptions (water, electricity, internet), depending on what the contract provides
  • Corrections and finishing work after handover
  • Home insurance

The written specification says what is supplied; whatever it does not mention is for you to pay. Read it before you sign, not at handover.

Running costs after the purchase

A home also costs money every year: co-ownership or residence charges, local taxes, insurance, maintenance, and management costs if you rent it out.

  • Co-ownership or residence charges (common areas, security, upkeep, shared facilities)
  • Annual local taxes, to be confirmed with your notary for your property
  • Insurance and routine maintenance
  • If you let the property: management costs, vacant periods, replacing furnishings

For a development with shared areas, ask the developer for a written estimate of the annual charges and how they will be managed after handover.

Building your budget, line by line

Add up the unit price, the deed costs, the financing costs, the cost of settling in and a reserve for the unexpected, then check that every line rests on a written document.

  • Unit price and what it includes: the developer's contract or offer
  • Deed costs: your notary's written breakdown
  • Financing: your bank's loan offer
  • Settling in: a quote or an estimate, based on the written specification
  • Annual charges: the written estimate from the developer or the managing agent
  • A reserve for the unexpected and for schedule slippage

If you are buying from abroad, add currency exchange and transfer costs, translation or legalisation of documents and, where relevant, the cost of a power of attorney.

Frequently asked questions

Are the notary's fees included in the price of a new-build?

Do not assume so: how the costs are split between you and the developer should be stated in writing in the contract or the offer. Ask your notary for a written breakdown.

Is the advertised price inclusive of taxes?

An advert does not always say. Ask the developer whether the price is exclusive or inclusive of taxes, and have the answer written into the contract.

When are the deed costs paid?

It depends on the stage: some costs are tied to the preliminary contract, others to the final deed. Ask your notary for a precise timetable of the sums to expect at each stage.

Should I keep a reserve for the unexpected?

As a precaution, yes: a schedule that slips, an option added or a currency movement can change the budget. The size of the reserve depends on your situation and your tolerance for risk; there is no single rule.
