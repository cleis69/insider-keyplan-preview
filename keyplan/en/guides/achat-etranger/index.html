Foreign buyer: what to have confirmed before buying a new-build in Morocco

KEYPLAN · Updated 9 October 2026

Where to start

Start by having a notary confirm that you can buy the kind of property you have in mind, before you reserve or pay any deposit.

The applicable rules may depend on your nationality, where you live and the type of property (finished home, off-plan unit, land). They are for a notary to confirm, not for a guide to settle: these are the points to put to them.

  • Can you acquire this type of property, and is any particular authorisation or formality needed?
  • Which documents will the notary ask the buyer for?
  • Which costs, taxes and reporting obligations apply in Morocco and in your own country?
  • How to pay, and what evidence to keep?
  • What happens to the property in case of death, separation or resale?

The steps of a purchase

The path is that of any off-plan purchase: choice, checks, reservation, preliminary contract, staged payments, handover and final deed, possibly with extra documents or checks for a foreign buyer.

  • Confirm with a notary that the purchase is possible for you (see above).
  • Choose a development and ask for the documents: land title, building permit, guarantee, contract.
  • Compare developments using the TRUST rating, then have a notary review the documents.
  • Reserve and sign the preliminary contract, in person or by power of attorney.
  • Pay by traceable transfers, following the schedule in the contract.
  • Follow the works, take handover, then sign the final deed.

Each step of an off-plan sale is explained in the VEFA guide; here the focus is on what specifically concerns the foreign buyer.

The documents you will need to prepare

Prepare your own file: a valid identity document, proof of address, family status and evidence of the origin of the funds. The exact list comes from the notary.

  • A valid passport
  • Proof of address in your country of residence
  • Civil status and family status documents, as applicable
  • Evidence of the origin of the funds (account statements, deed of sale of another property, etc.)
  • Translations and legalisation of documents issued abroad, if the notary requires them

Ask for the list in writing at the very first meeting: it avoids discovering a missing document the day before a signing.

Conversion into dirhams, origin of funds and borrowing

Your funds come from another country: allow for conversion into dirhams, for evidence of where they come from and, if you borrow in Morocco, for a bank-by-bank assessment depending on your profile.

  • Conversion into dirhams: compare the fees and the exchange rate applied by your bank.
  • Exchange rules applicable to your situation: to be confirmed with your bank and your notary.
  • Origin of funds: be ready to explain where the money comes from (savings, sale of a property, inheritance, gift) and keep the document that proves it.
  • Loan: ask banks whether they lend to foreign buyers, with what documents and what down payment.

The precautions for the transfers themselves (the account named in the contract, one transfer per payment call, evidence kept) are set out in the guide for MREs and apply to you too. Keep proof of payment and of the origin of funds in particular: they may be requested at the deed, on a resale or for your tax filings in your country of residence.

Tax in two countries, language of the contract, power of attorney, succession

Four topics deserve a written answer before you buy: tax in both countries, a power of attorney if you cannot attend, the language of the documents and what happens to the property on death. Each is for the relevant professional to settle.

  • Tax: what you will have to declare or pay in Morocco and in your country of residence, at purchase, while you own the property and on resale, is something to discuss with a tax adviser who knows both countries.
  • Power of attorney: if you cannot come to sign, a trusted person can do so in your name; the precautions to take are described in the guide for MREs buying from abroad and apply here too.
  • Language: if the contract is written in a language you do not read fluently, ask for a faithful translation before signing.
  • Succession: ask which law governs what happens to the property on death (Moroccan law, the law of your country of residence, or both). The questions about your spouse and marital property regime are listed in the guide for MREs.

Questions for a tax adviser who knows both countries

  • Which taxes and filings apply to the purchase, to owning the property and to its resale, in Morocco and in your country of residence?
  • Is there a mechanism that prevents being taxed twice on the same transaction, and how does it apply to your situation?
  • Which documents should you keep to establish the purchase price, the costs and the payments?

Ask each professional what they check and what falls outside their remit: a notary, a bank and a tax adviser do not have the same role.

Surrounding yourself with the right people

For a cross-border purchase, work with a notary, a bank and, if needed, a tax adviser, and keep all exchanges in writing.

A salesperson presents their own development; that is not neutral advice. For legal checks, rely on your notary, and keep the final say on every signature.

Use the TRUST rating to compare developments and prepare your questions, not to decide for you. It shows what has been checked and when; it covers neither your personal situation nor tax. Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner buy property in Morocco?

The conditions depend on the type of property and on your situation. It is the first question to put to a notary, before you reserve or pay a deposit.

Which documents should I prepare before seeing a notary?

Typically a valid identity document, proof of address, civil status documents depending on your situation, and evidence of the origin of the funds. Ask the notary for the exact list.

In which language will the contract be written?

That depends on the developer and the notary: ask which language the contract and its annexes are in before you reserve. If you do not read it fluently, ask for a faithful translation before you sign.

Does a foreign buyer follow the same steps as an MRE?

The off-plan purchase process is the same. What may differ are the formalities linked to your nationality and status, which the notary must confirm. The practical precautions on powers of attorney and transfers are in the guide for MREs.

Do I have to declare this purchase in my country of residence?

It depends on the law of your country of residence and on your situation. Ask a tax adviser in your country and keep the evidence of purchase, payment and costs.
